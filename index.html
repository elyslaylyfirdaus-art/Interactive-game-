Inspector's Grammar Manual

Describing Appearance & Personality

In advanced profiling, we use specific vocabulary rather than basic adjectives.

  • Complexion: Pale, tanned, dark, fair, olive.
  • Build: Slender, stocky, muscular, frail, well-built.
  • Nuanced Personality: Arrogant (too proud), Cynical (distrustful), Empathetic (understands others), Meticulous (pays attention to details).

Negative Prefixes (The Hard Rules)

Prefixes alter the meaning to its opposite. Master these specific combinations:

  • im- : Used before words starting with 'm' or 'p' (e.g., immature, impolite, impossible).
  • ir- : Used before words starting with 'r' (e.g., irresponsible, irrational).
  • il- : Used before words starting with 'l' (e.g., illogical, illegal).
  • in- : Often used with words starting with 'c', 'd', 't' (e.g., inaccurate, invisible).
  • dis- / un- : General use, but fixed to specific words (e.g., dissatisfied, unfamiliar, unreliable).

Present Simple vs Continuous (Stative Verbs)

Rule: Present Continuous is for actions happening NOW. Present Simple is for routines/facts. BUT watch out for Stative Verbs!

Stative Verbs (thoughts, senses, emotions, possession) are generally NOT used in continuous form.

  • Wrong: I am knowing the suspect.
  • Correct: I know the suspect. (Present Simple)

Exceptions (Tricky!): Some verbs change meaning.

  • "Think": I think he is guilty (opinion = Simple). / I am thinking about the case (action = Continuous).
  • "Taste": The poison tastes sweet (state = Simple). / The detective is tasting the coffee (action = Continuous).
